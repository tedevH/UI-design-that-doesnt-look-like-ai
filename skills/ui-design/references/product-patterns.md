# Product patterns

These patterns help choose what a screen should show and how it should behave. They are not visual templates. Start with the user's actual product, content, and audience; use a profile only as a prompt for better questions and a more relevant visual idea.

For every project, identify:
- the user's main task or decision;
- the information they need before they can act;
- the most meaningful object, state, or relationship to show;
- the interaction states that make the experience credible;
- the brand evidence that should shape the art direction.

Use the visual system from the project when one exists. When it does not, choose a direction that grows from the product's purpose and content, not from its category alone.

## Product and service patterns

### Marketing and SaaS
- **User need:** understand the product, trust its claims, and decide whether to try or buy it.
- **Show:** the working product, a real workflow, a useful example, or a specific result. Avoid a dashboard mockup that contains only invented charts and metrics.
- **Visual opportunity:** make the interface or workflow the centerpiece; show detail at a readable scale with context around it.
- **Avoid by default:** generic gradient hero, interchangeable feature-card grid, unexplained vanity metrics, and customer logos without permission.

### Analytics and operations
- **User need:** notice what changed, why it matters, and what action is available.
- **Show:** a believable data hierarchy, useful comparison, time context, filters, table or chart, and the state of one key task.
- **Visual opportunity:** use the data shape itself to create rhythm; let one decision-driving metric or anomaly take focus.
- **Avoid by default:** random dashboard tiles, unlabelled charts, overuse of color, false precision, and decoration that resembles telemetry.

### Finance and commerce tools
- **User need:** understand amount, risk, status, fees, and consequences before committing.
- **Show:** clear totals, comparison, dates, status, disclosures, and a reversible path where possible.
- **Visual opportunity:** make numeric hierarchy exact and calm; use consistent alignment, tabular figures, and restrained status color.
- **Avoid by default:** vague success claims, fake balances, pressure timers, ambiguous green/red status, and decorative charts without meaning.

### Learning and study
- **User need:** focus on material, practice recall, track progress, and know what comes next.
- **Show:** real lesson content or cards, current progress, a next action, and useful completion or empty states.
- **Visual opportunity:** treat learning material as a reading surface; make recall, progress, and feedback feel tactile and calm.
- **Avoid by default:** gamified badges without learning value, giant streak counters, and a timer that competes with the lesson.

### Health and care
- **User need:** find trustworthy information, complete a task, or feel safe asking for help.
- **Show:** clear service or care information, plain language, useful next steps, privacy expectations, and accessible controls.
- **Visual opportunity:** choose warmth or clinical precision based on the real service and audience; make reassurance come from clarity and credible detail.
- **Avoid by default:** unsupported outcomes, fake clinician portraits, vague wellness language, or color as the only status cue.

### Local business, food, and hospitality
- **User need:** decide whether to visit, book, or order.
- **Show:** actual location, hours, menu or service details, price, access, availability, and contact path.
- **Visual opportunity:** use real place, material, product, or process as a visual anchor; let typography and photography work together.
- **Avoid by default:** placeholder lifestyle photography, fictional awards, hidden prices, and generic “discover our story” filler.

### Product shops
- **User need:** inspect the item, compare options, and understand purchase details.
- **Show:** the actual product at useful angles or in context, price, variants, availability, delivery, care, and return details.
- **Visual opportunity:** give the object scale and breathing room; treat swatches, specs, and packaging as part of the system.
- **Avoid by default:** fake reviews, urgency claims, tiny product imagery, and variant controls that look interactive but do nothing.

### Creator, portfolio, and agency
- **User need:** assess the work and understand the creator's role and point of view.
- **Show:** the work itself, role, context, date, and outcome when known; make contact easy.
- **Visual opportunity:** let the projects define the page's color and rhythm; use a distinct index or case-study composition.
- **Avoid by default:** generic project thumbnails, unverified metrics, and a hero that takes more space than the work.

### Editorial and documentation
- **User need:** read, navigate, scan, and return to useful information.
- **Show:** clear title, author or context when relevant, section structure, figures, references, and wayfinding.
- **Visual opportunity:** use typography, measure, annotations, footnotes, and image cadence to make reading feel authored.
- **Avoid by default:** marketing layouts around long-form text, weak heading hierarchy, and decorative callouts that break reading flow.

### Events and communities
- **User need:** understand what is happening, whether it is for them, and how to join.
- **Show:** date, time zone, location or access link, schedule, participants, cost, and registration state.
- **Visual opportunity:** borrow from the event's actual subject, venue, or artifact; balance atmosphere with logistics.
- **Avoid by default:** stock conference visuals, invented speakers, and an RSVP button without event details.

### Utilities and creator tools
- **User need:** complete a focused task and understand the result.
- **Show:** inputs, default values, live result, constraints, copy/export path, and error states.
- **Visual opportunity:** make the input-output relationship the composition; turn the result into the focal object.
- **Avoid by default:** onboarding marketing around a one-screen utility and output that cannot be copied or used.

### Games and entertainment
- **User need:** understand the goal and feel feedback from play.
- **Show:** playable state, instructions, score or progress if relevant, pause/restart, and keyboard or touch affordances.
- **Visual opportunity:** let the core mechanic determine the visual language, motion, and sound; keep controls readable.
- **Avoid by default:** elaborate title screens that delay play, motion that obscures state, and decoration unrelated to the mechanic.

## Turn a pattern into an art direction

Do not copy the same composition each time a category appears. Translate the user's specific brief through this sequence:

1. Pick the primary task and the content that proves the page serves it.
2. Find one brand or product detail that can shape the visual premise.
3. Choose the focal form: product, image, data, typography, diagram, object, or scene.
4. Choose a composition that makes the focal form legible and memorable.
5. Choose a restraint: one aspect to keep quiet so the focal idea has room.
6. Check that the mobile composition preserves the same intent with an appropriate rearrangement.
